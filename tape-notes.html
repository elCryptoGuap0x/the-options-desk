The premium dies first. The idea can still be right.

This pamphlet is the free edge that does not belong to any desk: how short-dated options actually lose money. Twelve pages. No ticker. No setup to copy.

Use it for: reading a public ticket, deciding whether a contract is even tradable, and not turning one green print into a larger account.

Education / entertainment. Not advice. Not signals. Not this desk's live rules. Same-day options can go to zero.

01 · Two clocks

Stock clock vs option clock

The stock can grind your way for two hours and the contract can still be down. You rented a move by a time. If the move is late, small, or choppy, three things eat you before the thesis gets a vote:

  • Time — every quiet minute is rent.
  • Spread — you buy ask, sell bid. Mid is fiction.
  • Quote noise — a 0.3% stock dip can print −20% on a same-day contract.
Yield: if the underlying has not done anything useful in the first several minutes, you are not "early." You are paying rent on a dead impulse.

02 · The fill is the first P&L

No spread, no trade

What you seeWhat it means
Bid 1.40 / ask 1.42Tradable. Round-trip is pennies.
Bid 1.20 / ask 1.55You start −20% if you lift and dump.
Last 1.50, bid 1.10Last is a tombstone. You cannot exit there.
  • A limit sitting on a 40-second-old quote is a chase, not patience.
  • If you need a 30% contract win just to beat the spread, skip it.
  • Thin 0DTE singles whip more than SPY/QQQ. That is a filter.
Yield: name the bid and the ask out loud before you care about the chart.

03 · Tenor

0 / 1 / 2 days is three products

TenorWhat you actually bought
0DTEMinutes. Afternoon ≠ open. After 2pm ET this is a melting ice cube.
1DTEStill fast. Overnight is a second bet you may not have meant.
2DTEMore room. Still not a swing. Weekend is another product.

Holding to expiration is rarely the plan. If the idea needs "tomorrow" you bought the wrong contract — or you should be flat.

Yield: match hold time to tenor. A 3-hour thesis does not belong in a same-day option.

04 · Side is not mood

"Market is red" is not a put

A call pays only if that strike's premium rises after decay and spread. A put is the mirror. Color of the session is a backdrop, not a trigger.

  • Down 1% since the open can already be finished.
  • A second push that looks like the first is often the one that fails.
  • Index-tied names moving tick-for-tick with SPY are not "their own" trade.
Yield: trade a move that is happening or clearly failing. Do not invent a side from the day's P&L.

05 · Read a ticket in 20 seconds

Four fields, then the dots

  • Side — what the contract needed.
  • Minutes — how long the idea was allowed to work.
  • In → out — whether the fill was already a tax.
  • Contract % — never account %, never stock %.

Dots: green = option marked up that minute, red = down. A long red string right after entry usually means the impulse was over when the order landed.

Yield: 100% win rate on 1 ticket is "1 of 1." The week table is the adult number.

06 · The free edge

Most names should never print

Selectivity is not this desk's secret. It is available to anyone.

  • Already extended — pass.
  • Chop, no follow-through — pass.
  • Cannot name a tight market — pass.
  • Already in a name — pass. One position.
  • Need activity to feel like a trader — that is the leak.
Yield: twelve looks and one fill is a good morning. Zero fills can be a perfect day. Activity is not skill.

07 · Size

A winner is not permission

Same-day premium can lose half its value on a quote flick the stock barely felt. That is why size stays stupid-small until a large sample says otherwise — and a large sample is tens of tickets, not one green Wednesday.

  • Cap dollars, not "I like this ticker."
  • One contract on a $2.00 option is $200 at risk before the stop, not "one lot."
  • If the plan changes because the last chip was green, there was no plan.
Yield: do not add size to celebrate. Add size only after weeks of process + a real sample. Most people never get there. That is fine.

08 · Three exits

Hope is not a fourth

  • Working — lock some. Do not donate a winner back to noise.
  • Not working — several minutes with no new favorable extreme is information. Cut it.
  • Broken — if the underlying invalidates the idea, leave. Do not wait for the option quote to invent a reason.

Leaving extra on the table after a lock is normal. Riding a same-day winner "because it could go further" is how +20% becomes −30%.

Yield: decide the exit categories before the fill. Inventing them mid-trade is revenge with extra steps.

09 · Time of day

The session is not one market

WindowWhat usually happens
Open ~30 minRange + first impulse. Spreads widest. Easy to buy the climax.
MiddayChop. Premium rots. Most "I should do something" fills live here.
Last 90 min0DTE decay accelerates. New risk needs a better reason than boredom.

A name still red on the day at 1pm is not, by itself, a put. The move may have finished at 10:15.

10 · How losers actually look

Clean vs sloppy

Clean: tight contract, idea never worked, cut on time or structure, small dollars. That is tuition.

Sloppy: chased a late quote, wide spread, no stop, added size, held because "it should come back." That is how accounts end.

  • Never green after entry → usually late, not "unlucky side."
  • Green then round-trip with no lock → exit plan was missing.
  • Stock recovered, option did not → you learned the two clocks.
Yield: grade the ticket on process first. P&L is the loudest and worst teacher on n=1.

11 · Watch this site

A log is not a booth

The tape is often delayed on purpose. You are not supposed to front-run a robot. You are supposed to see what a small book actually did.

  • Judge: few tickets, tight contracts, fast cuts, small size.
  • Do not copy a fill. Do not scale off a 100% chip.
  • Tips buy nothing — no rules, no next trade, no chat.

Deeper issues exist only if someone asks: more on fills, more on losers, more on samples. Until then this is the whole free book.
