What you're looking at

A short pamphlet on short-dated options. Ten pages. No ticker. No "today." No next trade.

Education and entertainment only. Not investment advice. Not a signal service. The live tape on the desk is a log of one small account. This issue is the language. It does not change.

01

What this desk is

A public log of one account that trades short-dated options — contracts that expire the same day or within two days.

One position at a time. Long stretches with no ticket are normal. Flat is a state, not a failure.

02

Option is not stock

You are not buying the company. You are renting a bet that price moves enough, soon enough, to pay the contract.

The stock can be "right" later and the option can still lose. Time and the bid-ask spread take premium even when the idea was directionally fine.

03

0DTE, 1DTE, 2DTE

Those labels are days to expiration. Fewer days means faster decay and jumpy quotes.

These are not shares you hold through a thesis. Think minutes to a few hours. Holding to the bell is not the default plan.

04

Call versus put

A call wants the underlying up. A put wants it down. The badge on a ticket is the side.

The percentage on a ticket is the contract — not the stock, and not the whole account. A +20% contract on a small size is still a small dollar result.

05

How to read a ticket

  • Name and side (call or put).
  • Minutes held.
  • In and out, in contract dollars.
  • Percent on the contract.

Dots under a ticket, when they exist, are minute marks: green means the option marked up that minute, red means down. A replay is the same story compressed.

06

Most names never print

A ticker being up or down is not a reason to trade. Looking at many names and taking few is the job.

"Looked, passed" is a decision. Zero fills in a session can be correct. Activity is not skill.

07

Why size stays small

Short-dated options can lose most of their value quickly. Quotes can gap. Tools can fail.

One name. A cap on how much of the account sits in one contract. A green print is not a reason to raise size.

08

Exits exist on purpose

Winners get protected. Losers get cut. Time with no progress gets cut.

Holding to expiration is not the plan. Flattening later in the session is risk control. Leaving money on the table after a lock is normal, not a bug you "fix" by riding every winner to the low.

09

How to watch the site

The tape is a log. It is often delayed. It is not a live "buy this" feed.

A 100% win rate on one ticket is 1 of 1. The week table is more honest than a single chip. If you trade, you use your rules and your size.

10

What this is not

Not investment advice. Not a signal service. Not a proven system. Tips buy nothing — no rules, no next trade, no private chat.

Issues 01–04 exist only if someone asks: spreads and fills, reading a loser, time of day, samples and streaks. Until then, this is the whole free book.
