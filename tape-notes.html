Tape Notes · 00

The premium dies first. The idea can still be right.

Use this to read a public ticket, decide if a contract is even tradable, and not turn one green print into a larger account. Same-day options can go to zero.

Job: the language, not a strategy. Education / entertainment only. Not advice. Not this desk's live rules.

01 · Two clocks

Example · not a live ticket

The stock's clock and the contract's clock

Underlying
Option
Stock +0.4% · contract −18%. Different clocks.

The stock can grind your way for two hours and the contract can still be down. You rented a move by a time. If the move is late, small, or choppy, three things eat you before the thesis gets a vote:

  • Time — every quiet minute is rent (theta).
  • Spread — you buy the ask, sell the bid. Mid is fiction.
  • Quote noise — a 0.3% dip in the underlying can print −20% on a same-day contract.
Yield: if the underlying has not done anything useful in the first several minutes, you are not "early." You are paying rent on a dead impulse.

03 · Tenor

What you actually bought

0DTEmelts by the close
1DTE+ one overnight
2DTE+ a weekend, maybe

Minutes, not a swing. Afternoon on 0DTE is not the open. Holding to expiration is rarely the plan. If the idea needs "tomorrow," you bought the wrong contract — or you should be flat.

Yield: match hold time to tenor. A 3-hour thesis does not belong in a same-day option.

04 · Side is not mood

Trade the move, not the day's color

A call pays only if that contract's premium rises after decay and spread. A put is the mirror. Color of the session is a backdrop, not a trigger.

Down 1% since the open can already be finished. A second push that looks like the first is often the one that fails. Index-tied names moving tick-for-tick with the market are not "their own" trade.

Yield: trade a move that is happening or clearly failing — premium of this contract, not "market color."

06 · The free edge

Most names should never print

12 looks·1 fill
A good morning, not a slow one.
  • Already extended — pass.
  • Chop, no follow-through — pass.
  • Cannot name a tight market — pass.
  • Already in a name — pass.
  • Need activity to feel like a trader — that is the leak.
Yield: zero fills can be a perfect day. Activity is not skill.

07 · Size

Cap dollars, not tickers

$2.00 × 1 = $200 at risk
Not "one lot" — $200, before any stop.

Same-day premium can lose half its value on a quote flick the stock barely felt. Cap dollars, not "I like this ticker." If the plan changes because the last chip was green, there was no plan.

Yield: do not add size to celebrate. Add size only after weeks of process and a real sample. Most people never get there. That is fine.

08 · Three exits

Decide the categories before the fill

  • Working — lock some. Do not donate a winner back to noise.
  • Not working — several minutes with no new favorable extreme is information. Cut it.
  • Broken — if the underlying invalidates the idea, leave. Do not wait for the option quote to invent a reason.
Yield: inventing exit categories mid-trade is revenge with extra steps.

09 · Time of day

Same ticker, three products

Openwide spreads
Middaychop · rent
Last 90m0DTE melts

Open ~30 min: range + first impulse, spreads widest, easy to buy the climax. Midday: chop, premium rots, most "I should do something" fills live here. Last 90: 0DTE decay accelerates, new risk needs a better reason than boredom.

A name still red on the day at 1pm is not, by itself, a put. The move may have finished at 10:15.

10 · How losers actually look

Example · not a live ticket

Grade the process first

Clean cut — never green, cut on time
Sloppy round-trip — worked, then given back, no lock

Clean: tight contract, idea never worked, cut on time or structure, small dollars. Tuition.
Sloppy: chased a late quote, wide spread, no stop, added size, held because "it should come back."

Yield: grade the ticket on process first. P&L is the loudest and worst teacher on n=1.

11 · A log is not a booth

Watch, don't copy

The tape is often delayed on purpose. Judge few tickets, tight contracts, fast cuts, small size. Do not copy a fill. Do not scale off a 100% chip.

This issue is the language. Later issues are fills, tenor timing, and reading a loser. Not advice.

Glossary · Issue 00

Starred terms, in order

Premium
The price of the option contract. This is what you actually win or lose.
Theta
Daily (and intra-day) decay of that premium. Quiet minutes still charge rent.
Spread
Ask minus bid. You buy the high side, sell the low side.
Bid / ask
What you can sell at now / what you pay to buy now.
Mid
Halfway between bid and ask. A story, not a fill.
Last
Last reported trade. Can be stale. Not an exit.
Underlying
The stock or ETF the option is written on.
Quote noise
The option's bid/ask jumping without a real move in the underlying.
Impulse
A short, directional burst. If it is over, you are renting chop.
Round-trip
Buy then sell (or sell then buy). Both sides pay the spread.
0DTE / 1DTE / 2DTE (tenor)
Expires today / tomorrow / in two sessions.
Call / put
Contract that needs the underlying up / down enough, soon enough for this strike's premium to rise.
Climax
The last burst of a move, often the high or low of the push. Buying it as "the start" is the usual tax.
