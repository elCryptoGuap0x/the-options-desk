Tape Notes · 01

What you bought

An option is not a smaller version of the stock. It's a separate contract with its own price, its own clock, and its own way of dying. This issue is what that contract actually is, before anything about timing or exits.

Job: the instrument, not a strategy. Generic options mechanics — not this desk's rules. Not advice.

01 · Premium

The number you actually win or lose

The premium is the price of the contract itself — what you pay to buy it, what you receive to sell it. It moves based on the underlying's price, how much time is left, and how uncertain the next move looks. It is not the stock's price, and it does not move in a fixed ratio to it.

Yield: "the stock moved 1%" tells you very little about what the premium did. They're related, not identical.

02 · Call vs put

Two directions, two contracts

▲
Callwants underlying up
▼
Putwants underlying down
Both are bets that this contract's own premium goes up — a call needs the underlying higher, a put needs it lower.

A call and a put are opposite bets on direction, but the same mechanic: you own the contract, and it either gains value or it doesn't. There's no "being right about the stock" that pays you directly — only the contract's own price moving your way.

03 · Underlying vs contract

Two different things with one relationship

Underlyingthe stock or index itself
Contractthe option written on it

The underlying is the actual stock, ETF, or index. The contract is a separate, tradable thing that references it. The underlying can do exactly what you expected and the contract can still lose — size of the move, time left, and the contract's own price all matter, not just direction.

Yield: "I was right about the stock" and "the contract worked" are two different claims. Learn to tell them apart before anything else.

04 · Expiry

Every contract has a deadline

Same-dayhours left
Next-day+ one overnight
A few daysmore room, still short

Every option has an expiry — the date it stops existing. Nearer expiries move faster per dollar of underlying movement and decay faster if nothing happens. Further expiries are calmer but cost more up front. There's no free version that's both cheap and patient.

Yield: the expiry you pick is a real decision, not a technicality — it sets how much time your idea gets to be right.

05 · Reading what you own

Example · not a live ticket

Four questions before anything else

DirectionCall or put?
UnderlyingWhat does it need to do?
PremiumWhat did I actually pay?
ExpiryHow much time is left?

If you can't answer all four in a sentence, you don't fully know what you bought yet — regardless of whether the trade turns out to work.
