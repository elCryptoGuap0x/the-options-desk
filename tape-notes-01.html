Tape Notes · 01

The number on the screen is not the fill

Short-dated options do not lose only when you are wrong. They lose when you pay the market to get in and pay it again to get out. This issue is liquidity as a filter — not a ticker list.

Job: know whether a contract is tradable before you care about the chart. Education only. Not advice.

01 · Four prices

Last, mid, bid, ask

PrintUse
LastHistory. Can be a minute old. Not an exit.
MidStory. Nobody owes you this price.
BidWhat you can sell, now, if size exists.
AskWhat you pay to get long the contract.
Yield: mark-to-mid P&L is entertainment. Mark-to-bid is the money that can leave the account.

02 · Round-trip tax

Do the arithmetic before the click

Ask $1.60, bid $1.40. You lift one, dump one: −$20 per contract before the stock moves an inch. That is −12.5% on the $1.60 you paid.

  • Need +15% on the contract just to get to even? Skip.
  • Need +30%? That is not a trade. That is a donation with a chart.
  • Penny-wide on SPY/QQQ is a different product than a $0.40-wide single-name 0DTE.
Yield: spread ÷ ask is the first hurdle rate. If the idea cannot clear it fast, it is not an idea for this tenor.

03 · Size on the book

A tight quote with no size is a trap

Bid 1.50 × 1, ask 1.52 × 1. Looks liquid. One contract eats the quote. The next print is 1.35 / 1.70.

  • Check size on both sides, not just the pennies.
  • Your 2-lot can be the whole market on a thin strike.
  • After you buy, the bid you thought you had may vanish.
Yield: if your order is a meaningful fraction of displayed size, you are the liquidity. Price will move because of you, not the stock.

04 · Stale limits

Waiting is not the same as being patient

A working limit at "fair" mid while the ask walks away is how late fills happen. On 0DTE the walk takes seconds.

  • If it does not fill in a few seconds on a liquid name, the market already left.
  • Chasing the new ask is a different trade than the one you approved.
  • Cancel-and-replace mid-impulse is how you buy the high of the bar.
Yield: a missed fill is cheaper than a late fill. "I had to get it" is not a reason.

05 · Stops on thin paper

The option quote is not the stock

A 0.4% dip in the underlying can print a 20% hole in a same-day contract. A resting stop on the option can fire on that noise even if the stock thesis is intact.

  • Know whether you are stopped on the contract or on the underlying.
  • Wide option stops still die if the trigger is the bid flicking.
  • No resting stop at all is how a "quick look" becomes a disaster.
Yield: the first risk on 0DTE is often the quote, not the trend. Size as if the bid can vanish.

06 · Which names even have a market

No chain, no opinion

A beautiful chart on a name with Friday-only expiry, or $0.50-wide 1DTE, is a postcard. You cannot express it without paying a tax that eats the edge.

  • Index products: usually the tightest same-day paper.
  • Mega-caps: sometimes. Check the actual strike, not the brand.
  • Story names up 8% with no near expiry: watch, don't invent a contract.
Yield: liquidity is a gate. Chart quality does not override a dead chain.

07 · After you are in

Exit liquidity is worse than entry

Everyone wants the same door when it is working or when it is dying. The bid you saw at entry is not a promise.

  • Take the bid when you decided to leave. Mid-hope is how winners round-trip.
  • A limit to "get a better out" on a collapsing 0DTE is often a hold.
  • If size disappears, flatten at market. Pride is expensive.

08 · Checklist

Before any short-dated click

  • Bid and ask named. Size named.
  • Round-trip tax as a % of ask.
  • Can I exit 1 contract without moving the market?
  • Is the last print younger than a few seconds?
  • If this fills 15 seconds late, is it still the same trade?

If any answer is "I don't know," you do not have a price. Issue 00 is the language. This issue is the tax. Not a setup list. Not advice.
