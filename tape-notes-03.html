Tape Notes · 03

The session is three markets wearing one ticker

Open, midday, and last hour do not pay the same rent. Treating 10:00 and 2:30 as the same 0DTE product is how quiet afternoons get expensive.

Job: know which clock you are on before you call something a setup. Not a timetable to copy. Education only.

The session, at a glance

Three products, one ticker

Openrange, then a spike
Middayflat, rent
Last 90msteep decay
Same day. Not the same clock.

01 · The open

Example · not a live ticker

Range, impulse, widest quotes

Underlying, first 30 min
Range, then a spike — easy to buy the climax

First half hour: ranges print, first drives happen, spreads are often worst. A gap-and-go with no pause is usually extended before you finish the sentence.

Yield: the open is for recognition, not for proving you are awake.

02 · Midday

Where "I should do something" lives

Underlying + premium, midday
Underlying flat · premium decaying underneath it

Volume thins. Premium rots. The stock is still red or green on the day, so the brain invents a trade that should have been taken at 10:12.

  • Still down 1% at noon is not a fresh put.
  • A 15-minute coil in a dead tape is often just lunch.
Yield: midday default is pass. Activity here is usually a leak, not a missed calling.

03 · Last 90 minutes

0DTE is a different animal after lunch

Underlying flat · premium, last 90m
Flat stock, steep premium drop — the clock, not the tape

Same-day contracts decay faster into the close. A new idea now needs more follow-through, not less, because there is less clock left for it to be "a little late."

  • A 40-minute thesis does not fit in a 50-minute life.
  • Holding 0DTE into the bell is a second decision. Make it on purpose or flatten.

04 · Color of the day

The open is not the session

What you seeWhat it is not
Index red since 9:31A standing put for six hours
Name green +2%A standing call after the move is three hours old
VIX upPermission to size up

Day color is context. It does not reset the "is this impulse fresh" question every hour.

05 · News windows

The print is not the trade

Scheduled numbers create a burst. The burst is often done in the first minutes. Trading "because there is news" after the spike is late by definition.

  • If you did not have a plan before the number, you do not get one after.
  • Headlines arrive late on any public page. Do not use a delayed site as a news desk.
Yield: news explains why a range broke. It does not make a three-hour-old break fresh.

06 · Overnight and 1DTE

Holding past the session is a new bet

A 1DTE that is quiet at 3:50 ET is not "more room." It is inventory into a gap you do not control. Weekend is another product on top of overnight.

Yield: if the plan was an intraday burst, flatten. Do not discover you are a swing trader at 3:58.

07 · Flatten as a habit

Not fear. Inventory control.

Books that trade same-day paper pick a time when new risk stops and open risk gets worked off. The idea is not optional: you do not want a melting 0DTE babysat by hope.

Yield: "still looks good" at the end of the window is how leftovers become overnight accidents.

08 · Spectator clock

How to watch a quiet tape

  • Open: expect looks. Do not expect a ticket.
  • Midday: a long scanning stretch is the job working.
  • Late day: fewer new tickets is health, not a broken bot.

If a public desk is flat for hours, that can be correct. Issue 00 is the language. This issue is the clock. Not advice. Not a schedule to copy.
