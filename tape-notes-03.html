Tape Notes · 03

Two clocks

The underlying runs on one clock. The option runs on another. They usually move together. When they don't, this is the issue that explains why — and why "the stock did what I thought" is not the same claim as "the trade worked."

Job: one idea, in depth. Generic mechanics, not this desk's rules. Not advice.

01 · The two clocks

Example · not a live ticket

Same event, two readings

Underlying
Option
Underlying grinds slowly up. Option is red every minute. Different clocks, same eight minutes.

The stock's clock measures price. The option's clock measures price and time and the tax of trading it. A move that's real by the stock's clock can still be a loss by the option's.

Yield: "the stock did what I expected" and "the trade worked" are two separate claims. This issue is about the gap between them.

02 · Time is rent

Example · not a live ticket

Theta doesn't wait for a verdict

Underlying
Option
Underlying goes nowhere. Option bleeds anyway — quiet minutes still charge theta.

A flat stock is not a neutral outcome for a short-dated contract. Every quiet minute is rent paid whether or not the idea eventually plays out.

Yield: "it hasn't moved yet" is not the same as "nothing is happening." Time is always happening.

03 · A small dip, a big hole

Example · not a live ticket

Quote noise vs. a real move

Underlying
Option
A small wobble in the underlying can print a much bigger swing in the option — quote noise, not a real reversal.

Same-day contracts amplify small underlying moves into large premium swings, especially on a thin book. Not every red dot means the thesis broke.

Yield: before reacting to an option's own wiggle, check whether the underlying actually did anything.

04 · Why the stock's clock can win and yours can still lose

Three things sit between the two clocks

  • Time — the move has to happen soon enough to matter before expiry.
  • Size — the move has to be big enough to clear the spread and still be worth something.
  • Path — a move that arrives the "long way," with a dip first, can shake you out before it pays.
Yield: being right about direction is necessary. It has never been sufficient.

05 · What "early" actually means

Early is not the same as patient

If the underlying has not done anything useful in the first several minutes, holding is not "being early" — it's paying rent on a move that hasn't started. Genuinely early looks like a fresh, small move just beginning. Falsely early looks like a flat line and a hopeful story.

Yield: a thesis that needs "just a little more time" every few minutes is usually a thesis that already failed quietly.

06 · Reading a ticket through two clocks

The question behind every closed ticket

When a ticket closes red, the first useful question is not "was I wrong," it's "which clock actually killed this." The underlying can be blameless and the contract can still die on time or on the spread. All three are real outcomes, and they call for different lessons.

07 · Reading a real divergence

Example · invented numbers, not a live ticket

Walking through one, start to finish

Underlying
100.00100.40
Call
1.501.22
Underlying: 100.00 → 100.40, up 0.4%. Call: 1.50 → 1.22, down 18.7%.

The underlying did move the right way — up, all session, no reversal. The call still lost almost a fifth of its value. Why: the move was small relative to the strike, it took most of the session to arrive, and theta was charging the whole time it waited. Right direction, wrong size, wrong pace.

Yield: "the underlying went the right way" is the beginning of reading a ticket, not the end of it.

Glossary · Issue 03

Starred terms, in order

Theta
Daily (and intra-day) decay of an option's premium. Quiet minutes still charge rent.
Quote noise
An option's bid/ask moving without a proportionally real move in the underlying.
