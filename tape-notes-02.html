The fill is a tax

Short-dated options do not lose only when you are wrong. They lose when you pay the market to get in and pay it again to get out. This issue is liquidity as a filter — not a ticker list.

Job: know whether a contract is tradable before you care about the chart. Generic mechanics, not this desk's rules. Not advice.

02 · Round-trip tax

Example · not a live ticket

Do the arithmetic before the click

$1.40exit bid
$1.60paid ask
−$20 / −12.5% before the stock moves an inch
spread ÷ ask = hurdle rate

Need +15% on the contract just to get to even? Skip. Need +30%? That is not a trade. That is a donation with a chart. Penny-wide on a liquid index is a different product than a wide single-name 0DTE.

Yield: if the idea cannot clear the hurdle fast, it is not an idea for this tenor.

04 · Stale limits

Example · not a live ticket

Waiting is not the same as being patient

1.52→1.58→1.64
Ask walks right over three ticks. Your limit is still sitting at 1.52.

A working limit at "fair" mid while the ask walks away is how late fills happen. On a same-day contract the walk takes seconds. If it does not fill in a few seconds on a liquid name, the market already left.

Yield: a missed fill is cheaper than a late fill. "I had to get it" is not a reason.

05 · Stops on thin paper

Example · not a live ticket

The option quote is not the stock

A small dip in the underlying can print a much larger hole in a same-day contract.

A resting stop on the option can fire on that noise even if the stock thesis is intact. Know whether you are stopped on the contract or on the underlying.

Yield: the first risk on a same-day contract is often the quote, not the trend. Size as if the bid can vanish.

06 · Which names even have a market

No chain, no opinion

no near-dated expiry listed — dead chain

A beautiful chart on a name with a distant next expiry, or a very wide next-day quote, is a postcard. Index products: usually the tightest same-day paper. Single names: sometimes — check the actual strike, not the brand.

Yield: liquidity is a gate. Chart quality does not override a dead chain.

07 · After you are in

Example · not a live ticket

Exit liquidity is worse than entry

1.48 ×12→1.22 ×1
The bid you saw at entry is not a promise.

Take the bid when you decided to leave. A limit to "get a better out" on a collapsing same-day contract is often a hold. If size disappears, flatten at market. Pride is expensive.

08 · Checklist

Before any short-dated click

  • Bid and ask named. Size named.
  • Round-trip tax as a % of ask.
  • Can I exit 1 contract without moving the market?
  • Is the last print younger than a few seconds?
  • If this fills 15 seconds late, is it still the same trade?

If any answer is "I don't know," you do not have a price. Issue 01 is the instrument. This issue is the tax. Not a setup list. Not advice.

Glossary · Issue 02

Starred terms, in order

Size (on the book)
Contracts advertised at that bid or ask. ×1 is a postcard, not a market.
Mark-to-mid
Valuing the position at the midpoint. Feels better than what you can actually sell.
Liquidity
Ability to enter and exit at a known tax. Tight pennies with no size is fake liquidity.
Resting stop
A live order sitting at the broker that can fill without you watching.
Chain
The list of expiries and strikes that actually trade. No near expiry means no trade.
Flatten
Close the whole position. Flat means no option risk left.
